Preflight checks content before it goes out. Every claim in a draft is compared with the
company's own approved guidelines, facts and past content. When we can prove a problem,
we show the proof and suggest a fix that keeps what the writer meant. When we can't prove it
either way, a person decides.
1. Problem definition and evidence
Most growing companies have brand guidelines, but in practice nobody checks content against them. Writing
now comes from employees, agencies and AI tools, and mistakes like a wrong claim, an old price, off-tone
wording or an off-brand image often reach the public before anyone spots them. The surveys below come from
vendors and trade press, so read them as a rough guide, but they all tell the same story:
• 85% of organisations have brand guidelines but only about 30% enforce them consistently; 77% regularly
deal with off-brand content (Marq).
• 47.1% of marketers meet AI inaccuracies several times a week and 36.5% say incorrect AI content has
already gone public (NP Digital, 565 US marketers).
• 50% of consumers prefer brands that avoid GenAI in consumer-facing content; Gartner advises labelling AI
use (1,539 US consumers).
Most tools look at how a piece of writing sounds. We also check what it says: is each claim true, is it current,
and does it match what the brand has already published?

1.2 Root causes behind the failures
1. Guidelines are written for people, not machines. A rule like "keep a friendly but professional tone" can't be
checked the same way twice, so enforcement depends on who happens to review.
2. Facts have no owner and no expiry date. Prices, customer counts and policies change, but old copies stay
in decks, posts and web pages and nobody is told.
3. Feedback comes late and without a reason. Problems show up after publishing or after several review
rounds, so people start working around review.

1.5 What a good solution has to do
1. Catch problems before publication, not after.
2. Base every judgement on the organisation's own approved material, so results can be checked.
3. Explain each issue and offer a fix that keeps the writer's purpose, so people learn and don't work around it.
4. Hand anything uncertain or risky to the right person, with the evidence ready.
5. Keep confidential material inside the organisation and say clearly when AI or an outside service is used.

A creator's journey
1. Priya, a marketer, uploads a draft and fills in a short intent form: purpose, audience and two must-say
points.
2. Preflight returns four findings in under a minute. Two are verified, one is a contradiction with the returns

3. She accepts the fix for the policy error and edits the suggested rewrite of the superlative.

same claim is told straight away.
6. Her manager approves the new version, a Brand Passport is saved, and Priya publishes.

What makes us different, in one line: instead of judging how content sounds, Preflight checks what it says
against the company's own approved facts and its own published history, and every human ruling becomes
knowledge it can reuse. We haven't seen these ideas emphasised together:
• Findings that can't exist without a cited source passage: no source, no violation.
• Contradiction checks against the brand's own past content.
• Claim Shelf-Life: facts have owners and expiry dates, and the owner is warned before one lapses.
• Intent Lock: every fix comes with a note on what it keeps and what it drops.
• Guideline Debugger and Rule Simulator: guidelines get tested and improved like any other asset.
• Brand Passport: a tamper-evident record for each approved asset, including whether AI was used.

Metric Target Measured by

Demo story: a creator writes "Free returns within 14 days". Preflight puts that next to the uploaded policy (page
3, version 2, which says 30 days), marks it Contradicted and suggests a fix that keeps the message. The

evidence for it, so it goes to legal as a question instead of being called wrong. After 50 drafts the dashboard
shows one rule being overridden 70% of the time, which tells the owner it needs clearer wording.
